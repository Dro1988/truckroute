Why Google Maps Still Doesn't Have Truck Mode (and What Truckers Actually Use)

October 12, 2026

Ask any trucker: the most dangerous button in the cab is "Start navigation" on an app that doesn't know you're 70 feet long.

The gap

Google Maps — the app nearly everyone drives with — has no truck mode for consumers. No height clearances, no weight limits, no hazmat routing, no truck parking. Google does offer large-vehicle routing, but only as a paid fleet API for enterprises, not in the app drivers actually use. So millions of professional drivers navigate with software designed for a Honda Civic.

The result is predictable: bridge strikes, weight-station surprises, and 30-point turns on roads a truck should never have entered.

What truckers actually use today

  • Paid truck GPS apps ($20–30+/mo) with dedicated truck routing — the professional standard, priced like it.
  • Free car nav + local knowledge — works until it doesn't, usually at 2 a.m. in an unfamiliar town.
  • CB radio and truck-stop lore — still real, still useful, not a routing engine.

The missing middle

There's never been a truck nav app that's actually affordable for an independent driver: free to start, a few bucks a month for premium — undercutting the $20–30 incumbents — with the truck-specific features drivers ask for most: low-clearance warnings, weigh-station status, truck parking, and real reports from drivers on the road ahead.
